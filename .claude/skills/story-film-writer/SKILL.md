# Story Film Writer

The script decides whether a film lands; polish never rescues a weak idea. Run this skill before style-director, ad-engine or any Seedance prompt. Nothing is generated until the script passes the gate in step 5.

It is built from what worked and what failed for Dw:
- **Worked:** "FLMLNK Milk" (Vox-style incongruity explainer, Dw's best to date) and Higgsfield's viral animal short "Living with people". Both run on the same engine: **incongruity theory** (section 0).
- **Failed:** FLMLNK "Zero Views" and "Unseen" v1. They were polished but felt like ads: two layers of metaphor, blame on an abstract system, 2-second shots, slogans.

## 0. The engine: incongruity, then resolution

People notice, laugh at and remember what doesn't fit, and they feel smart when the mismatch suddenly makes sense. Every script is built on that two-step:

1. **Incongruity:** put two things together that don't belong. Movies and milk. A bear working in a diner. A deer in a cinema. The mismatch is the hook; it must land in the first 3 seconds.
2. **Resolution:** the mismatch reveals a truth the viewer hadn't seen. "The industry treats films like they expire." "These animals never chose to live with us."
3. **Flip:** the ending breaks or reverses the pairing ("Movies aren't milk." / "Did we have a choice?"), so everything before it reads differently.

**Rules for the pairing:**
- **Invent a fresh pairing for every script.** Milk is a worked example, not a template; never reuse a pairing from an earlier film unless Dw asks.
- **Make it everyday on one side.** At least one half must be something everyone already understands with no explanation (milk expires; bears don't fit in porta-potties).
- **Use only one pairing per film.** Two incongruities compete; a pairing that needs decoding (animals standing for films standing for filmmakers) is a failure, not an incongruity.
- **It must resolve to the core** (section 2). If the mismatch is only funny and doesn't reveal the belief or wound, find another.
- **Generate 5 to 8 candidate pairings,** score each on surprise (how unexpected) and resolution (how instantly the truth clicks), and show Dw the top three before writing.

## 1. Pick the mode

| Mode | When | Engine of feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Incongruity explainer** (default for FLMLNK and B2B) | The audience is an industry with a belief to change: distributors, investors, partners | A pairing that exposes a belief the viewer holds, knocked down, then hope |
| **B. Story film** | Broad audience, a universal human pain | Laugh, then a turn, then grief or recognition |

Ask Dw only if the audience or goal is unclear. Otherwise pick, say which, and go.

## 2. Find the core (both modes)

Write one sentence before anything else:
- **Mode A, the belief:** the wrong idea the audience acts on every day. FLMLNK example: "films expire like milk; if people didn't find it the first time, they never will."
- **Mode B, the wound:** a pain a stranger already knows and feels. Examples: losing your home, giving years to something nobody sees. Never an industry problem; nobody grieves for a catalog.

Ground it in reality: Dw's rule is "dark and accurate", built on real cases.

## 3. Mode A: the incongruity explainer

The steps below are the structure. The examples in quotes come from Milk; replace them with lines that belong to the new pairing.

1. **The pairing, stated in the first 3 seconds.** Milk example: "Movies = milk." One everyday thing everyone already understands. One layer only.
2. **Show the habit in the audience's own words.** "Release it. Give it a little attention. See what happens. Then make room for the next." They should recognize themselves without being attacked.
3. **The crack: why it's strange.** "Which is strange when you think about it, because movies don't spoil." Audiences and platforms changed; give one concrete before/after with specific years ("a film that missed in 2018 might land in 2026").
4. **Proof from worlds they already know.** Three or four quick, familiar examples: an old song going viral, old sneakers turned collectible, old games cool again, the restaurant nobody noticed with a line around the block. Then the pivot: "But with film?"
5. **State the belief out loud,** then lock it away: "If people didn't find it the first time, they probably never will." (Archive: do not disturb.)
6. **The brand enters as the action, not a tagline.** The logo breaks the archive open, then the method in three plain verbs: go back in, ask who might care now, test it.
7. **Concede a limit.** "Not every old movie deserves another marketing dollar. We're not trying to save everything." This is where trust is earned; never skip it.
8. **Reframe the failure.** A checklist of what went wrong: product ✓, timing ✗, marketing ✗, right people ✗. Land on "Nobody put it in front of the right people."
9. **Flip the pairing.** Milk example: "Movies aren't milk." Then hope: "Some are still waiting for their audience. We intend to find them." Logo and URL, nothing else.

**Craft for Mode A:** one idea per screen; captions build word by word with the voice; paper cut-outs or tactile objects on one palette; constant small motion; specific years and named objects instead of statistics; 90 to 110 seconds; a calm, confident voice.

## 4. Mode B: the story film

1. **Keep it literal.** Characters are themselves, never stand-ins for products. Zero decoding.
2. **Make the viewer complicit.** The cruelty comes from people on screen in their own words ("Who's going to watch that?", "You don't belong here"), never from type or "the system".
3. **Laugh first, for about half the runtime.** Three characters or attempts in the same pattern (interview line, a scene from their day, a small humiliation). Each gag runs 6 to 20 seconds with a human reacting.
4. **Split interview lines around the gag:** first half, cut to their day, back for the second half. Write the pause into the line ("is good. And... comfortable.").
5. **One question flips everything,** asked by the unseen interviewer. Then 1.5 to 2 seconds of black and silence. The answer is four to six words and recasts every joke.
6. **One long held reveal.** No music, one diegetic sound tied to the cause.
7. **End on one real, sourced fact on black.** The brand comes last and quiet, as a watermark or logo card.

## 5. Gate: score before any credits are spent

Score each 1 to 5. Every score must be 4 or higher, or rewrite.

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Core | Does the belief or wound feel instantly true to the target viewer? |
| Incongruity | Is the pairing surprising, fresh (not reused), and does it resolve to the core within seconds? |
| One layer | Is there zero decoding? Is there one pairing only, with one everyday side? |
| Recognition | Do viewers see themselves (their habit, or their cruelty)? |
| Proof | Is it shown through things they already know, or a real fact, never a claim? |
| Restraint | Does it concede a limit or avoid overclaiming? |
| Turn | Does the ending flip the opening and change how everything before it reads? |
| Not an ad | Would it still work with the logo removed? |

Show Dw the core sentence, the top three pairings, the logline, the opening line, the turn line and the scores before writing shots. Offer two or three options when the core is weak.

## 6. Writing the lines

- Short, plain, spoken. One thought per line. Read every line aloud.
- Understatement beats statement. No line states the theme in poster language.
- Specific beats general: years, objects, a line around the block.
- Captions are the script; write them to build word by word.
- Write the closing line first, so everything sets it up.

## 7. Hand-off format

- Beat-by-beat with timecodes, the voice-over or dialogue in quotes, and every visual described physically.
- Mark held shots and silence with seconds.
- Keep on-screen graphics to captions, the pairing's visual system and the end card. No lower-thirds, caption bars or slogans unless Dw asks.
- Then hand off to style-director, ad-engine and higgsfield-seedance-prompt (Mode B) or launch-motion or hyperframes (Mode A cut-out style).

## 8. Honesty

- Facts on screen must be real and sourced; give Dw the source.
- No invented FLMLNK results, testimonials or numbers. Proof is felt, not stated, unless Dw supplies the figure.
- Borrow structure from references, never their content, characters or lines.
